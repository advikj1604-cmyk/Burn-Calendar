    How the two records are made comparable

    The problem

    MODIS (1 km pixels, from late 2000) and VIIRS (375 m pixels, from 2012) do not see the same fires. VIIRS picks up many small and cool fires that MODIS misses, and more satellites fly now than before. If you simply add the counts together, burning seems to jump when a new sensor starts, even if nothing changed on the ground.

    The method

    1. Keep presumed vegetation fires only. Drop MODIS detections with confidence below 30 and VIIRS detections marked low confidence.
    2. Count detections per grid cell, per sensor, per week (52 bins a year).
    3. For weeks when two sensors were both flying, measure how many detections each sensor makes for every one that VIIRS S-NPP makes. This is done per grid cell and per calendar month, because the mix of fire types changes with place and season. Cells with little data lean on the regional value.
    4. Burning activity for a week is the total count from all sensors flying that week, divided by the sum of their detection rates. The unit is VIIRS S-NPP equivalent detections.
    5. Weeks when the sensors in orbit had too little detection power are left out of the harmonized record.

    Limits to keep in mind

    • Activity means satellite detections, not area burned or number of fires. One large fire gives many detections.
    • Fires that MODIS cannot see at all cannot be recovered. The method assumes the share of such fires in a cell and month stayed the same over the years.
    • Years before VIIRS rest on fewer detections, so they are noisier.
    • Cloud and smoke hide fires from every sensor. Quiet weeks in a cloudy season may be undercounted.
    • Overpass times differ (Terra in the morning, Aqua and VIIRS in the early afternoon), which the monthly rates only partly absorb.
